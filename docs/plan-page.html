Game plan · updated Nov 13, 2026

Playnes

A real-world flying game for a 5-year-old airplane fan. The player runs a little charter company from Tri-Cities and Charlotte and flies real business jets across North America. A friendly co-pilot teaches the basics, and good flights earn Wings that unlock new planes.

02Principles

Built for a 5-year-old

Pre-reader first

The co-pilot speaks every instruction, and every button is a big icon. Text is only there for grown-ups.

Real things, kid-sized

Real airports, planes and words like "rotate", "gear up" and "butter landing". The physics is simplified and protects the player.

Never stuck, never punished

No crashes, no game over, and Wings are never lost. After two misses the co-pilot helps more, and after three the co-pilot shows how.

Short and fair

Flights take 5–8 minutes. Every unlock has a clear price. No timers, streaks, ads or random rewards.

03A flight

Tri-Cities to Charlotte, step by step

Charter card: “The Lopez family is going to a soccer game in Charlotte. 4 passengers.”

  1. Walkaround30 s
    Tap the glowing tires, the fuel cap and the red REMOVE BEFORE FLIGHT flag.
  2. Taxi to Runway 2345 s
    The co-pilot makes the radio call, the tower answers, and the player follows the yellow line.
  3. Takeoff30 s
    Eighty knots… ROTATE! Pull back gently. Positive rate, gear up!
  4. Climb & turn45 s
    Fly through big hoops and bank toward the magenta course line.
  5. Cruise above the clouds90–240 s
    The cruise is sped up. The clouds open over a real landmark, Lake Norman on this trip. Keep the passenger's juice cup from spilling.
  6. Approach60 s
    Flaps, gear down, then follow the glide-path hoops. Two red, two white, you're just right!
  7. Touchdown & debrief60 s
    A landing under 150 feet per minute scores BUTTER! and three stars. Then Wings, a passport stamp and a tower-camera replay.

iPad

  • Big touch yoke on the left and a notched throttle lever on the right
  • Optional tilt-to-fly, holding the iPad like a steering wheel
  • Bluetooth game controllers work too

Windows PC

  • Arrow keys or WASD steer, Page Up/Down sets the throttle, G for gear, F for flaps
  • Xbox controller
  • Touchscreen laptops get the iPad layout

The physics is honest where it counts: turn rate comes from bank angle and speed, so big jets turn wide. It also protects the player. Stalls are prevented, bank is limited, and a "Ground Buddy" warning (Terrain! Pull up!) gently pulls the plane up before it reaches the ground.

Earning Wings

  • Charter flight: 10, plus 1 per 50 nm (up to 30 more)
  • Landing stars: +5 each
  • No spilled juice: +5
  • New airport stamp: +15
  • Lessons: +25 or +50

Pacing

  • First jet in the first session
  • A new plane every 2–4 sessions after that
  • About 35–40 sessions to fill the hangar
  • Wings also buy paint, stickers and hangar toys

Left out on purpose:

  • Real-money purchases
  • Ads
  • Loot boxes
  • Daily streaks
  • Energy timers
  • Limited-time offers
  • Guilt messages
08Devices

Safety, iPad and PC

Child safety

  • No accounts, chat, links, ads or tracking
  • Settings sit behind a parent gate
  • Optional "time to land" session timer
  • Tip: iPad Guided Access locks the iPad to the game

iPad specifics

  • Installs to the Home Screen as an app, with no browser bar to tap away
  • Saves are protected, plus parent backup codes
  • Large touch targets, landscape layout
  • Quality adjusts to keep the frame rate smooth

10Risks

Design review

  1. HighGoogle's terms and children's apps
    Google Maps Platform Terms §3.2.1(c)(vi): no use “in a Customer Application that would be deemed to be a ‘Website or online service directed to children’ under … COPPA.”

    COPPA's definition is generally understood to cover commercial services, so a private, non-commercial family game is arguably outside it. This is not legal advice, and Google has the final say. The game stays private, and the map source can be swapped to Cesium ion with one setting.

  2. MediumiPad performance

    Photoreal 3D is heavy. Mitigations: the above-the-clouds cruise, fog, a capped tile cache, adaptive resolution, and a basic-globe fallback.

  3. MediumNo offline flying

    Google forbids offline use of its map data. Without Wi-Fi, the game shows a friendly "need Wi-Fi to fly" screen, and the hangar still works. An offline cartoon world is planned for Phase 4.

  4. LowSaved progress on iPad

    Safari can clear website data. Installing to the Home Screen, persistent storage and backup codes keep the hangar safe.

  5. LowPlane names and models

    Real names are fine for a private game. The 3D planes are built from scratch in a simple toy style, so no model licensing is needed.

  6. LowAirport data accuracy

    The crowd-sourced runway data had errors at CLT. Each game airport should be checked against the official FAA diagram.
